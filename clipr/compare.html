stands against OpusClip

One is a shipping product used by iHeartMedia, Univision, Billboard and HubSpot. The other is a production core with 265 passing tests and no users. This compares them honestly, including the places the incumbent is simply further along.

What ClipR is

An autonomous clip desk for live channels.

It attaches to a stream the moment it goes live, ranks moments in real time, cuts and captions the winners, and posts them to every account the creator owns — usually before the stream has ended. The creator's involvement is optional and mobile: approve or kill, on a phone, in the twenty minutes after they stop streaming.

The bet is narrow and specific. Clipping tools price by the minute of source video you feed them, which suits a 40-minute podcast and punishes a six-hour stream. ClipR meters monitored hours and published clips instead, so the stream you threw away costs nothing and the clip you rejected is free.

Native upload where the API allows it; a staged post and a push notification where it doesn't.

What is actually built: the schema and workspace isolation, the detection cascade, the eval harness and regression gate, plan and entitlement logic, the job queue, publishing adapters, safety screening, the cost ledger, the analytics taxonomy, and a readiness audit that computes itself from evidence. 265 tests, including 23 that run against a live Postgres.

What is not: the web app, the worker loop that ties the stages together, the Twitch and TikTok integrations in production, and a single user. The comparison below is a comparison of designs and prices, not of shipping products.

First, two corrections

Seeing the real pricing page made Opus look stronger, not weaker.

The earlier teardown was built from third-party write-ups, because the session could not reach opus.pro directly. Two claims in it were wrong, and both errors flattered us.

Auto-posting is not the upgrade lever

I wrote that Starter withholds publishing, and that this does most of the $15 → $29 upgrade work. The live page shows auto-post to YouTube Shorts, TikTok and IG Reels sitting in the $15 Starter plan. What Pro adds is the scheduler — queueing a month of posts — which is a far softer lever than I claimed.

The API is not fully enterprise-gated

Pro carries Limited API Access; Business carries full API and custom integrations. They have also shipped an MCP server and an "Agent Opus" product. The agentic surface is a place they are ahead of us, not behind.

And they do target streamers. "Livestreamers" is one of ten named verticals in their own Solutions nav. This is not a segment they overlooked — it is one they serve with a meter built for somebody else. That is a narrower and more honest version of the wedge than "nobody serves streamers."

The honest read

What each side would actually win on.

Where OpusClip wins today

  • It exists. Real customers, real logos, real revenue. Every row above is a design claim on our side and a shipped feature on theirs.
  • Breadth. B-roll, dubbing, thumbnails, speech enhancement, XML export. A creator who wants one tool for everything wants theirs.
  • Price, right now. Pro at $14.50/mo undercuts our $29 entry outright, and $15 Starter already auto-posts.
  • The agentic bet. API, MCP and Agent Opus are live. We have none of it.
  • Distribution. They are buying the search term that brought you to this comparison.

Where ClipR would win, if it shipped

  • The meter. A streamer's cost stops scaling with hours of footage they never wanted clipped. That is structural — Opus cannot copy it without repricing every existing customer.
  • Zero-touch. No upload step. If the creator has to remember to do something, the product has already failed.
  • "Rejected clips are free." It makes our ranker being wrong our cost instead of theirs. It is also the single best line in the pricing.
  • Unlimited destinations at entry. Streamers run a main, a clips channel and a burner. Six connections is a ceiling; ours costs us nothing.
  • Degrading instead of dying when a platform pulls API access — the risk most likely to end either company.

The one-line version: Opus is a better video tool. ClipR is a better deal for someone who is live thirty hours a week — and that is the only customer it should try to win. Competing on features would be losing on their terms; the meter is the whole argument.

The unresolved question is price. Their $14.50 promo against our $29 entry means the pitch has to be worth a 2× premium on day one, which it only is above roughly 15 streamed hours a month. Below that, they are simply cheaper and we should say so.
